Hi. I'm Undmg.
I make things
people play & use.

I build games on Roblox, small tools that save people time, and the odd experiment for the web. Almost everything starts the same way: I want something, it doesn't exist yet, so I make it.

Most of my work is in Luau (Roblox's version of Lua), Python and plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. I also use AI tools like Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini every day. Not so they do the thinking for me, but so I can move faster: I break a problem into pieces, explain it clearly, and check what comes back.

My rule is simple: ship it, then make it better. A finished thing that people can actually use beats a perfect thing that only lives on my computer.

01 Roblox game · Live

Blokd!

Drag, drop, clear. A block puzzle game where the fun is in your hands, not just your head.

What is it?

You drag block pieces onto an 8×8 board. Fill a whole row or column and it clears. Clear several in a row and you build a combo, and combos are where the big scores are.

Why I made it

I wanted a puzzle game you play on feel. Quick eyes, quick hands, and that “in the zone” rhythm, instead of sitting there thinking for ages.

What's inside

  • Solo — beat your own best score.
  • Co-op — up to 4 friends share one board and take turns.
  • Duels — 1v1, and your clears send junk blocks to your rival.
  • Chaos Mode & events — frozen blocks, lava, conveyor belts, even bosses.
  • Style — 20 themed worlds, 30+ block skins, 60+ emotes.
  • Progress — daily quests, a season pass, a prestige shop and leaderboards.

02 Roblox game · Demo

Goldngine

An arena shooter that moves like it’s 1998. Fast, twitchy and all about how you move.

What is it?

A free-for-all arena on Roblox where you bunnyhop, air strafe and rocket jump around the map. Everyone spawns with every weapon, ammo is scattered around, and when you die you’re back in the fight in seconds. No timer, just drop in and play.

Why I made it

Roblox movement feels floaty next to the old PC shooters I grew up on. So I rebuilt that kind of movement from scratch in Luau, with the same speeds, friction and air control, and built a game around it.

What's inside

  • Movement — bunnyhop, air strafe, surf ramps, crouch jumps and ladders.
  • 14 weapons — crowbar, shotgun, crossbow, a guided rocket launcher and more.
  • Free-for-all — no time limit, 2 seconds of spawn protection.
  • Stations — health and armor chargers, plus medkits and batteries.
  • Lobby — a practice range with target dummies and no PvP.
  • Extras — gibs, explosions and an in-game developer console.

03 Roblox tech demo · For developers

Pistonart

The way cars drove in Need for Speed: Most Wanted (2005), rebuilt on Roblox and open for anyone to test.

What is it?

A test drive, not a full game. You get a car, a whole city and a few tools, so players and developers can try out the driving system I built: how the car grips, slides, boosts, sounds and feels at 200 mph.

Why I made it

Most Roblox cars feel floaty because they lean on Roblox’s built-in physics. I wanted the weight and drift of an old favourite, so I went to the source: I read the game’s own handling numbers and rebuilt the whole car from scratch in Luau to match them.

What's inside

  • Physics — my own suspension, tire grip and drift, running 120 times a second.
  • Engine & gears — engine sound that follows the revs, a real rev limiter, nitrous.
  • Feel — chase camera, tire smoke, skid marks, nitrous flames and sparks.
  • Open world — downtown, autumn hills, a port, a mountain pass and a highway, all built by code.
  • Multiplayer — see everyone’s cars with their wheels, smoke and engine sound.
  • Extras — a street circuit with a lap timer and a car colour picker.

04 Web experiment · Greek myth × weirdcore

Oracle.exe

In short: the Oracle of Delphi lives inside a haunted old computer. Type any question and she answers with a cryptic prophecy. Ask the same thing twice in a day and you get the same answer, like a real oracle.

Why: in ancient Greece people travelled for weeks to ask one question. I wanted that feeling in a browser tab, with eyes in the sky, pop-ups that ask if you're still there, and a start menu that won't let you shut down.

05 Web experiment · "Now" page

Golden Hour

In short: my "now" page, built like a video game main menu. A girl sits on a rooftop above a hazy golden city; the menu shows what I’m working on, my devlog and my setup.

Why: I wanted a page that's nice to just sit on. The city and rooftop are real 3D, she's a 2D drawing sitting inside it, and the lo-fi radio is original music generated live in your browser. Everything in the menu updates from one small file.

06 Desktop app · Open source

RBXL to Text Tool

In short: Roblox saves games in a file that AI tools can't read. This app opens that file and pulls out every script and object as plain text you can paste anywhere.

Why: I wanted AI to help with my Roblox code, but it couldn't see inside my game. I built this for myself, it turned out useful, so I made it public. It exports to TXT, LUA or LUAU and works with Rojo.
